Drums

Plaits' 808- and 909-style drum voices and Peaks' FM drum, as single-voice components.

Every drum works the same way:

  • init(sampleRate), then setters (all 0..1, except frequency in Hz).
  • setAccent(x), then trigger() on each hit.
  • process() on every sample. It returns about ±1 at full accent, louder for some settings.
  • setSustain(true) plays a continuous tone instead of hits. Plaits does this when nothing is patched to its trigger input, and it makes the drums usable as oscillators.

The pattern for a trigger input:

dsp::SchmittTrigger trig;   // member

if (trig.process(inputs[TRIG].getVoltage(), 0.1f, 1.f)) {
    drum.setAccent(inputs[ACCENT].getVoltage() / 10.f);   // 0..10 V accent CV
    drum.trigger();
}
outputs[OUT].setVoltage(5.f * drum.process());

Settings take effect at once, including in the middle of a ringing hit, so you can modulate them with CVs.

AnalogKick

#include "pt/drums/analog_kick.h" · from Plaits (analog bass drum)

A circuit model of the TR-808 bass drum. A trigger pulse strikes a resonant filter tuned to the drum's pitch, and the filter's ringing is the drum. A second pulse briefly raises the pitch (the 808's attack "knock"), and the drum can push its own pitch up with its level (self-FM) for a punchy downward sweep. Plaits adds the self-FM and the tone and decay ranges beyond the original 808.

SetterEffect
setFrequency(hz)Pitch. 808 kicks sit around 40–60 Hz; toms go higher.
setTone(x)Brightness, and how much click leaks through.
setDecay(x)Ring time, from a short thud to a long boom.
setAttackFm(x)Pitch knock at the attack.
setSelfFm(x)Pitch follows level: punchier, with a pitch drop.
setAccent(x), setSustain(on)As above.
pt::AnalogKick kick;
dsp::SchmittTrigger trig;

void onSampleRateChange(const SampleRateChangeEvent& e) override { kick.init(e.sampleRate); }

void process(const ProcessArgs& args) override {
    kick.setFrequency(pt::voltToHz(inputs[VOCT].getVoltage() - 2.f));   // 0 V = C2 (65 Hz)
    kick.setTone(params[TONE].getValue());
    kick.setDecay(params[DECAY].getValue());
    kick.setAttackFm(params[PUNCH].getValue());
    kick.setSelfFm(params[PUNCH].getValue() * 0.5f);
    if (trig.process(inputs[TRIG].getVoltage(), 0.1f, 1.f)) kick.trigger();
    outputs[OUT].setVoltage(5.f * kick.process());
}

AnalogSnare

#include "pt/drums/analog_snare.h" · from Plaits (analog snare drum)

A circuit model of the TR-808 snare, extended:

  • Shell: resonant filters ("modes") struck by a pulse. The 808 has two; above two-thirds of the tone range, three more inharmonic modes fade in.
  • Snares: rectified noise through a band-pass, with its own envelope.
  • snappy balances the two.
SetterEffect
setFrequency(hz)Shell pitch, around 150–250 Hz.
setTone(x)Below 2/3, the 808's two modes from round to bright. Above, extra metallic modes.
setDecay(x)Ring time of shell and snares.
setSnappy(x)0 is all shell (a tom-like knock), 1 all snares (noise).
setAccent(x), setSustain(on), seed(s)As above; seed repeats the noise exactly.
snare.setFrequency(180.f * pt::semitonesToRatio(params[TUNE].getValue() * 12.f));   // ±1 octave
snare.setTone(params[TONE].getValue());
snare.setDecay(params[DECAY].getValue());
snare.setSnappy(params[SNAPPY].getValue());
if (trig.process(inputs[TRIG].getVoltage(), 0.1f, 1.f)) snare.trigger();
outputs[OUT].setVoltage(5.f * snare.process());

SynthKick

#include "pt/drums/synth_kick.h" · from Plaits (synthetic bass drum)

A digital kick in the style of the TR-909 and its imitators:

  • The body is an oscillator whose waveform morphs from a clean sine to a jittery, soft-clipped triangle (dirtiness).
  • A fast pitch envelope gives the punchy start.
  • A model of a transistor VCA shapes the level.
  • A click plus filtered noise make the transient.
SetterEffect
setFrequency(hz)Pitch.
setTone(x)Brightness, and the level of the click and noise transient.
setDecay(x)Body decay.
setDirtiness(x)Clean sine (0) to a gritty triangle (1). Fades out at high pitches.
setFmAmount(x), setFmDecay(x)Depth and length of the initial pitch drop.
setAccent(x), setSustain(on), seed(s)As above.
// A 909-ish kick with one "punch" knob driving the pitch envelope
float punch = params[PUNCH].getValue();
kick.setFrequency(50.f);
kick.setFmAmount(punch);
kick.setFmDecay(0.2f + 0.3f * punch);
kick.setDecay(params[DECAY].getValue());
kick.setDirtiness(params[GRIT].getValue());
kick.setTone(0.3f);
if (trig.process(inputs[TRIG].getVoltage(), 0.1f, 1.f)) kick.trigger();
outputs[OUT].setVoltage(5.f * kick.process());

SynthSnare

#include "pt/drums/synth_snare.h" · from Plaits (synthetic snare drum)

A model of the TR-909 snare:

  • Drum: two soft-clipped triangle oscillators with a pitch drop at the start. The 909's quirk of the oscillators slightly resetting each other is kept; it gives grit at high FM and low pitch.
  • Snares: band-passed noise, with an envelope that holds for 40–70 ms before decaying. The hold is the 909's body.
SetterEffect
setFrequency(hz)Drum pitch, around 150–250 Hz.
setFmAmount(x)Pitch drop at the attack.
setDecay(x)Decay of drum and snares.
setSnappy(x)Drum (0) to snares (1), equal-power.
setAccent(x), setSustain(on), seed(s)As above.
snare.setFrequency(pt::voltToHz(params[TUNE].getValue() - 1.f));   // around C3
snare.setFmAmount(params[FM].getValue());
snare.setDecay(params[DECAY].getValue());
snare.setSnappy(params[SNAPPY].getValue());
if (trig.process(inputs[TRIG].getVoltage(), 0.1f, 1.f)) snare.trigger();
outputs[OUT].setVoltage(5.f * snare.process());

HiHat

#include "pt/drums/hihat.h" · from Plaits (hi-hat engine)

A TR-808 hi-hat and a ring-modulated variant, chosen in init():

  • HiHat::SQUARE_808: six square waves at the 808's inharmonic ratios, through the 808's lopsided VCA. The classic tick and sizzle.
  • HiHat::RING_MOD: three ring-modulated oscillator pairs, through a clean VCA with a two-stage decay (an open hat that chokes into a short tail). Harsher and more electronic.

Both are coloured by a band-pass and end with a high-pass. noisiness blends in plain clocked noise for washier, cymbal-like sounds.

CallEffect
init(sampleRate, model)Model as above. Default SQUARE_808.
setFrequency(hz)Base pitch of the metallic oscillators; about 400 Hz is the 808.
setTone(x)Filter colour, from dark to sizzling.
setDecay(x)Closed (short) to open (long).
setNoisiness(x)Metallic (0) to plain noise (1).
setAccent(x), setSustain(on), seed(s)As above.
// Closed and open hats from one voice: the open trigger uses a longer decay
pt::HiHat hat;   // onSampleRateChange: hat.init(e.sampleRate, pt::HiHat::SQUARE_808);
dsp::SchmittTrigger closedTrig, openTrig;

hat.setTone(params[TONE].getValue());
if (closedTrig.process(inputs[CLOSED].getVoltage(), 0.1f, 1.f)) {
    hat.setDecay(0.2f);
    hat.trigger();
}
if (openTrig.process(inputs[OPEN].getVoltage(), 0.1f, 1.f)) {
    hat.setDecay(0.75f);
    hat.trigger();
}
outputs[OUT].setVoltage(5.f * hat.process());

FmDrum

#include "pt/drums/fm_drum.h" · from Peaks

A sine-wave drum with pitch envelopes, noise and overdrive. One voice covers kicks, toms, zaps and noisy snares. Its morph(x, y) control sweeps through Gillet's presets on an X/Y pad, which is how Peaks' two-knob mode works.

How it works. A sine is swept down in pitch by an FM envelope and by a short fixed "thump" envelope, which adds weight to low notes. The output also feeds back slightly into the pitch. An exponential amplitude envelope shapes the hit. The noise control mixes in white noise above 0.5 and applies tanh overdrive below it. Peaks did all this in 16-bit fixed point; this port is float throughout.

CallEffect
setFrequency(hz)Pitch, about 33 Hz to 1 kHz.
setFmAmount(x)Depth of the pitch sweep (up to 96 semitones).
setDecay(x)Length of the amplitude and pitch envelopes.
setNoise(x)Below 0.5 overdrive (most at 0); above 0.5 noise (most at 1); 0.5 is clean.
morph(x, y, snare)Sets all four from Peaks' presets: snare = false for kicks and toms, true for snares and zaps.
trigger(), seed(s)As above. FmDrum has no accent or sustain mode.
// Peaks-style two-knob drum: X/Y morph, a switch for kick or snare presets
fm.morph(params[X].getValue(), params[Y].getValue(), params[SNARE_MODE].getValue() > 0.5f);
if (trig.process(inputs[TRIG].getVoltage(), 0.1f, 1.f)) fm.trigger();
outputs[OUT].setVoltage(5.f * fm.process());
// Or set it directly: a zappy tom
fm.setFrequency(110.f);
fm.setFmAmount(0.6f);
fm.setDecay(0.4f);
fm.setNoise(0.3f);   // a little overdrive
